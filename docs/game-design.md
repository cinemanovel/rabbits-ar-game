# Game Design

The game should feel like a restrained mystery unfolding through fragments, places, rituals, and community interpretation.

## Design Tone

- Ambiguous, not confusing.
- Cinematic, not theatrical.
- Premium, not busy.
- Strange, not random.
- Earned discovery, not constant reward spam.

## Core Loop

The likely long-term loop is:

1. Receive or discover a signal.
2. Interpret the clue.
3. Visit, scan, decode, or observe something.
4. Unlock a fragment.
5. Compare meaning with the community.
6. Progress the larger mystery.

Do not overbuild this loop early. Prototype the smallest version that proves it feels compelling.

## Puzzle Rules

- Puzzles should have clear inputs and fair feedback.
- Avoid puzzles that require unsafe travel, trespassing, nighttime isolation, or private property access.
- Provide accessible alternatives for players who cannot visit a location.
- Do not rely on obscure external knowledge unless the clue teaches the player where to look.
- Track puzzle state explicitly in data. Do not infer important progress only from local storage.

## Progression

- Favor chapters, cases, fragments, signals, and discoveries over generic levels.
- Progress should feel like uncovering a story, not grinding points.
- Avoid daily streak pressure unless it fits the fiction and respects player wellbeing.
- Rare discoveries are good; permanently missable core story is risky.

## Community Discovery

Community should help players connect fragments, verify theories, and feel scale. Community should not become an unmoderated content firehose.

Until moderation is mature, prefer controlled community surfaces:

- Theory prompts.
- Curated dispatches.
- Limited reactions.
- Staff-authored summaries.
- Opt-in events.

## Content Authoring

Future story content should be data-driven where practical. Avoid hard-coding puzzle copy, chapter metadata, coordinates, or unlock requirements directly inside screen components.
