# Design References

These references describe mood and restraint. Do not copy protected layouts, logos, characters, or exact visual assets.

## Visual Mood

- Modern museum exhibit systems.
- High-end streaming title sequences.
- Declassified files and redacted documents.
- Night photography, glass, reflections, and low light.
- Signal analysis tools and field equipment.
- Minimal cinematic sci-fi interfaces.

## Product Feel

The app should feel closer to a premium mystery service than a casual arcade game. It can be strange and playful inside the fiction, but the shell should remain composed.

## Useful Reference Qualities

Look for references with:

- Sparse composition.
- Strong negative space.
- Subtle texture.
- Quiet typography.
- Limited accent color.
- Controlled reveal pacing.
- Physical-world mystery cues.

## Avoid

- Generic fantasy RPG UI.
- Cartoon inventory systems.
- Loud gamified reward explosions.
- Social media feed clones.
- Crypto or hacking cliches.
- Horror gore aesthetics.
- Random symbols without story logic.

## Applying References

When using a reference, translate the underlying quality into project tokens or components. For example, use the idea of redaction as a text treatment or reveal pattern, not as a full copied document design.

Every new visual pattern should answer:

1. What player state does this communicate?
2. How does it support mystery?
3. Can it be reused without becoming noise?
